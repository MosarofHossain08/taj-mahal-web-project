The Taj Mahal

The Taj Mahal is An immense mausoleum of white marble, built in Agra between 1631 and 1648 by order of the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan in memory of his favourite wife, the Taj Mahal is the jewel of Muslim art in India and one of the universally admired masterpieces of the world's heritage. It is famous around the world for its beauty and perfect symmetry, and it is often described as a symbol of love.

Quick Facts

Design

This clever architectural choice ensures that in the event of a structural collapse, the towers would fall away from the main mausoleum, protecting the central tomb from damage

White marble construction of Taj Mahal

The Taj Mahal is primarily constructed of white marble sourced from Makrana, Rajasthan. The marble’s reflective quality changes its colour throughout the day, appearing pink in the morning, white at noon, and golden at sunset.

The myth of the black Taj

A popular myth that Shah Jahan planned to build a "Black Taj Mahal" across the Yamuna River as his own mausoleum. However, this second monument was never constructed, possibly due to Shah Jahan’s imprisonment by his son.

History & About

When Was It Built?

Construction started in 1632 (after being ordered in 1631). Over 20,000 workers and artisans spent 16 years building the main white marble tomb,finishing it in 1648. It took another five years to complete the surrounding gardens and buildings, meaning the entire complex was officially finished in 1653.

Why Was It Built?

It was built out of deep grief and love. In June 1631, the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan’s favorite wife, Mumtaz Mahal, died while giving birth to their 14th child. Devastated by her death, the emperor stepped back from royal duties, gave up music and fine clothes for two years, and commissioned this grand mausoleum in Agra to honor her memory and serve as her final resting place. Later on, Shah Jahan himself was buried there beside her.

Key Architectural Features

  • White marble: The main building is made of white marble that seems to change color during the day.
  • Central dome: A large dome sits on top of the tomb, surrounded by smaller domed pavilions.
  • Four minarets: Four tall towers stand at the corners of the platform.
  • Pietra dura inlay: Flowers and patterns are made by setting colored stones into the marble.
  • Calligraphy: Verses from the Quran are written in black stone on the main archways.
  • Charbagh garden: A garden divided into four parts by water channels, with a long reflecting pool.
  • Perfect symmetry: Almost every part of the complex has a matching part on the opposite side.
